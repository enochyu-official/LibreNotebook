0.1 Proving and Problem Solving Tips

Throughout studying and solving olympiad style problems, I noticed and came up with some patterns and tips that I think could be helpful. I am not a olympiad coach or anything, but maybe I could share some of my thoughts and topics that I heard that quite surprised me.

0.1.1 Proving Inequalities

Inequality is definitely one of my favorite section, and I think there are many components that makes a solution elegant. Particularly, I think solutions that makes inequality feel natural are very elegant. Here is what I mean. Instead of trying to “solve the inequality” by making the best out of the conditions, I think “proving the inequality” in a natural way is more elegant. Say we have a constant on the right-hand side and we want to prove it. Some “bad proof” could include brute forcing and trying to figure out the meaning of the inequality just by looking at the constant.

It is, however, very important to deduce hints from the constants because they could reveal something very important like 2025 KMO Problem 6, which is included in Section ??. However that problem is also elegant in a way that it naturally uses the condition to prove the inequality without brute forcing. In short, I think solutions are elegant when we could deduce like this is true and that is true, then this gives the constant. Here is an example to manifest my point.

Exercise 0.1.1: 1997 KMO Problem 4 (\( \bullet \circ \circ \))

Given positive \(a, b, c\) such that \(a + b + c = abc\), show that \(\sum _\text {cyc} \frac {1}{\sqrt {1 + a^2}} \leq \frac {3}{2}\).

Solution.

I particularly like this problem because there are few ways of solving this, but the standard and famous solution deduces the constant from the left-hand side.

Consider the following equation for \(\triangle {ABC}\). \[ \tan C = \tan {180^\circ - (A + B)} = -\tan (A + B) = - \frac {\tan A + \tan B}{1 - \tan A \tan B} \] In other words, \(\tan A + \tan B + \tan C = \tan A \tan B \tan C\). Without loss of generality, let \(0^\circ < A, B < 90^\circ \). Notice that because \(c\) depends on \(a\) and \(b\) and \(\tan A\) and \(\tan B\) spans positive reals, we can let \(a = \tan A\), \(b = \tan B\), and \(c = \tan C\). Moreover, we could notice that \(C \in (0^\circ , 90^\circ )\) since \(c > 0\).

Continuing, consider the following inequality by Jensen. \[ \sum _\text {cyc} \frac {1}{\sqrt {1 + \tan ^2 A}} = 3 \sum _\text {cyc} \frac {\cos A}{3} \leq 3 \cos \left ( \frac {A + B + C}{3} \right ) = \frac {3}{2} \] Therefore, the inequality holds.

I think what makes this problem particularly elegant is the fact that we could transform the given conditions to different forms and we could use that to find the constant. Meaning, we could have derived this problem from scratch without even looking at the right-hand side.
